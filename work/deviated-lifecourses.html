Forum theatre · 2021–2023

Des vies déviées

Can a room of academics and university leaders recognise their own biases, without being lectured? Two commissioned forum-theatre shows for the University of Lausanne, performed by researchers who are also performers.

Role
Casting, writing, training, direction, MC
Commissioned by
UNIL Office of Equality & Centre LIVES; re-run for HEC Lausanne
Format
Short play + forum theatre (2022); forum only (2023), in French
Cast
7 performers and a helping hand (2022); 5 performers (2023)

The brief

In July 2021, the University of Lausanne’s Office of Equality and the Centre LIVES asked us to show how implicit (or unconscious) biases, such as sexism, racism and bias based on appearance, shape researchers’ careers over time. The events were open to everyone, though most of the audience came from the university: academics, staff and leadership. Biases concern everyone, and some in the room had lived their effects.

They had worked with a theatre company before. They came to us, The Catalyst, a science-entertainment collective, because we were researchers ourselves and knew that world from the inside. After a first meeting to understand their needs, we set three goals:

  • show the biases we have;
  • show their impact;
  • show what can be done.

Our first offer was a one-hour improv show. As their content took shape, we refined it with them into the format below, with a project plan running to the performance in June 2022.

The format

Both events opened with talks by a professor and other guests on the research behind the topic. Our part brought it to life. In 2022, it started with a 15-minute play, developed through improvisation and set in a world where the gender order is reversed. Seeing familiar situations flipped makes them easier to recognise, and harder to defend. In 2023, at HEC, we ran the forum only.

Then came the forum theatre, from the Theatre of the Oppressed tradition. A scene of everyday discrimination is played once. Then it’s played again, and the audience can stop it and propose a different way to respond. In 2022, in a large theatre with a big audience, most people stayed in their seats and suggested a variation, which the performer playing the victim then tried out live.

Casting

This was a fully open casting, so I could shape the ensemble. I looked for current or former researchers with stories to tell, some of them from minorities, and allies willing to stand with them. Some were already performers; others needed a nudge and took on supporting roles. I cast before writing, so the scenes could be tailored to the people playing them.

My first session is always about building the ensemble: trust, then vulnerability. A cast has to trust each other before they can be vulnerable together, let alone in front of an audience.

Writing and rehearsing

I wrote the show from brainstorming sessions where cast members drew on their own experiences, refined over rounds of feedback with the client, a few trusted sounding boards (including one of the cast, who carried the lead role in the forum), and two mentors with different styles. Three rehearsals over two and a half days trained the cast in improv, particularly character, relationships and status, so they could stay agile as the script kept evolving. Building on that shared language of trust and vulnerability, one session was devoted to a double skill: playing the villain convincingly, and facing one. Without that work, performers pull their punches, and the scenes don’t feel real enough for anyone to react, especially when the point is to show oppression.

Between rehearsals I had one-on-one calls with each cast member, from 30 minutes to two hours, to check how they were living with the material and to go through the show together. Meetings on site with the client and the technical team let us plan the space, and gave us beautiful lighting.

On the night

I MC’d both performances. I warmed up the room and set up a safe space, framed each part, and asked rhetorical questions to get people thinking before inviting them in. The room was heavy, and people were hesitant at first. Some sat near their own hierarchy, sometimes the very people the scenes were about, which made stepping in harder. One person was frustrated not to be handed a solution. That is the point of forum theatre, which builds answers together rather than handing them out; we talked afterwards, and I pointed her to the 5D bystander-intervention methods.

What I learned

Casting for lived experience worked. The audience responded strongly because the performers had lived these situations, and several people, including people in leadership roles, told us afterwards they had recognised themselves in the scenes. That’s powerful, and it has to be handled with care. From our debriefs:

  • Match the format to the goal. In a large audience, people rarely dare to step up. In a small workshop, they play. A show reaches more people; a workshop changes more.
  • Give the forum time. People were just warming up when we had to close.
  • Iterate early, then freeze, and budget an extra rehearsal to do it. Prototyping found the show’s direction, but the script kept changing until the last day. The cast adapted, but it would have felt safer to fix things earlier.
  • Don’t let it all rest on the victim. In forum theatre, the audience never replaces the oppressor, and the oppressor doesn’t pull their punches, so a quick fix is rare. When someone does find a strong response, often someone trained, we celebrate it, then explore other ways to respond: testing other methods, for example, or acting as a bystander.

What it left behind

The client invited us back for a second performance at HEC Lausanne in 2023, for an audience of professors and school leadership, in a more informal classroom setting with the chairs in a storytelling arc. They also introduced us to another institution. We met once, but I told them I likely couldn’t run it: doing it well would have meant taking time to understand that institution’s own context, and with my thesis and several cast members leaving Geneva, I couldn’t give it that. The scripts and method are documented, so the show can be revived in French or English, or adapted to a new context.
