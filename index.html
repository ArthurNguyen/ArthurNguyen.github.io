Arthur Nguyễn

A neuroscientist as much at home in a dataset as in a room full of people, and someone who gets things done.

Now: Workplace Quality of Life, Geneva University Hospitals (HUG).

For eight years, at the Brain & Learning Lab, University of Geneva (Campus Biotech), I ran behavioural and EEG research on flow in video games.

Alongside research, I create, and act in, site-specific immersive experiences, from Geneva’s Musée d’Art et d’Histoire to the campus where I worked. I also directed forum-theatre shows for the University of Lausanne. I perform improv theatre, sometimes from the piano, and run workshops now and then.

Research and experience design turn out to share a method: step into people’s shoes, turn what you notice into a clear intention (a hypothesis, or a mission statement), then test it with real people.

Room 1

How I work

Three threads, woven together

Most projects start with the first, need the second and end with the third. In practice they overlap all the time.

01Understand

Behavioural research · data · insight

Observe first. Then ask careful, precise questions, gather clean data, and draw honest conclusions.

  • Noticed, time after time, that spectators missed a mark that should have ruined my card trick. Turned it into an experiment, now published in Scientific Reports.
  • Treated participants’ experience as part of the method: people who feel welcome and safe give cleaner data. 170+ people went through my EEG study that way.
  • Built analysis pipelines that run end to end and regenerate clean, consistent reports, so others can check and re-run them.

02Design for and with people

Facilitation · storytelling · experience design

Listen and collect stories, spot people’s strengths, then cast them where they fit and are needed, or find what connects them. Design experiences that are safe to step into and fun to stay in.

  • Wrote and directed participatory work: forum theatre for the University of Lausanne, immersive theatre at the Musée d’Art et d’Histoire, a Halloween ARG for my campus.
  • Co-led the Social Campus Biotech Association, building a lively community across a campus of several buildings, with a monthly event for 150+ people.
  • Mentored 9 junior researchers, and coached many people in public speaking, including a short session before a colleague’s successful CHF 830k Ambizione grant pitch.

03Get it done

Planning · problem-solving · delivery

Plan ahead, see problems coming, lighten the load. Move fast, stay calm under pressure, and keep it joyful.

  • Since 2011, delivered events and projects with museums, universities and associations, often with volunteer teams and tight timelines, and learned how much bigger machines run from the inside, including at Disneyland Paris and the Montreux Jazz Festival.
  • At Google, spotted a manual routine the team had simply got used to. Keen to make our days easier, I wrote shell scripts, then an Excel version for colleagues less comfortable with the command line, so the repetitive part came off everyone’s plate.
  • Designed and built Line Up, a party game for my lab’s retreat, in under two days with Claude: a clear intention first, then fast iteration on what players would touch.

Back room

On the side

Curiosity and play

Both run through everything I do; improv is where they get the most room today, and where I sharpened my storytelling, teamwork and trust in the process. It built on what was already there: listening from music, MC’ing from Disney, and an ease with not knowing what comes next. I’ve performed seriously since 2022, in English and French: with The Murderet, an improv troupe that also let me deepen my immersive work, and with The Living Room, my own project. I’ve also directed shows, played piano as musical director, run the tech booth, hosted jams, taught workshops, and brought international trainers to Geneva to enrich the local scene.

Piano: classical, then jazz, then music production. My niche: sight-reading, accompanying (chamber music, or shaping a song’s structure live for an improvising singer), and improvising a score under a scene.

Escape rooms: 120+, including some of the world’s best, and I travel for them, as I do for immersive theatre. Until you’ve played a great one, it’s hard to believe how premium the experience can be. Half hobby, half design research.

Also close-up magic (once for science), lion dance, origami and the occasional dive.
